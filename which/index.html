How tools are chosen

  • Free tools only. A tool with a paid upgrade is listed only if the free part does the job on its own.
  • A pick should be: free, usable without an account, not tied to one maker's catalogue, usable on a phone, open about its formulas, and likely to stay online. Few tools meet all six, so the tags and the notes say where a pick falls short.
  • Where nothing neutral exists, a tool tied to one maker may be the pick. It is tagged, and the note says so.
  • A task with no free tool I would point a colleague to is left off the list.
  • Each of these is my pick, not "the best". "Also try" lists other tools for the same task, with one plain fact about each.
  • The tools were opened and dated, not tested for accuracy. Check any result that matters against the maker's data, the standard or a hand calculation.
  • The date on each row is when its link was last opened. A row not rechecked for more than six months says so.
  • No payment, sponsorship or affiliate links. I have no affiliation with any publisher listed. Two of the tools are my own and are tagged "my own tool".
  • Links go to other people's sites, which can change or close without notice. No text, images or logos are copied from them. To report a broken link, use "report broken" beside it. To suggest a tool, or to ask for a correction or removal, use "Suggest a tool" at the top of the page.

What the tags mean

one maker's products
The tool only works with, or only recommends, one manufacturer's products.
needs account
You have to sign in before you can use it.
needs email
It asks for an email address before it gives a result.
download
Free software you install, not a web page.
reference, not a calculator
A source document to read.
my own tool
Built by me, on this site.

No tag means free, in the browser, with no sign-up seen.
